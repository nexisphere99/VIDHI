## LOCATION: KATRAJ PG ROOM & BUILDING

### NPC: ROHIT SHARMA   Morning Wake-Up Banter

**[Trigger: Player interacts with rohit_bed between 06:00-08:00]**

Rohit emerges from sleep the way a submarine surfaces   slowly, reluctantly, with a great deal of mechanical groaning. His cot protests as he rolls onto his back, and for a moment he just lies there, one eye open, assessing whether the world has improved overnight.

"Bhai," he croaks. "Kitne baje hain?"

"Six forty-five."

"Kyun?" He says it like I've personally offended him by being awake. "Krishnan ki class gyaarah baje hai. GYAARAH. That's four hours. Main char ghante mein aur teen baar so sakta hoon."

"Lecture das baje hai, Rohit."

He stares at the ceiling. Processes this. "Fuck."

He sits up   a production involving three separate attempts, each accompanied by a grunt that suggests his body is staging a labor dispute. His basketball jersey is twisted, one sleeve around his neck. He scratches his beard, yawns wide enough to display dental work, and reaches for the protein shake bottle on his bedside table. Takes a swig. Winces.

"Yeh kal raat ka hai." He looks into the bottle with the expression of a man who's just made a terrible discovery. "Khatta ho gaya."

"Toh mat pi na."

He shrugs and takes another swig. "Protein protein hota hai, bhai. Fresh ya expired."

This is the man I share eighteen square meters with. This is my best friend. God has a sense of humor and it's extremely specific.

"Chal, bathroom mein chal," he says, heaving himself vertical. "Tera woh fancy shampoo use karun? Mera khatam ho gaya."

"Rohit, woh conditioner tha."

"Same thing."

"It literally isn't."

"For me it is. Mere baal ko distinction samajh nahi aata."

### NPC: PG LANDLORD SHARMA (No relation to Rohit)

**[Trigger: Player interacts with landlord_door between 09:00-21:00]**
**[Location: pg_stairs]**

Sharma uncle opens the door in his undershirt, a towel draped over one shoulder, reading glasses perched on a nose that's seen sixty years of Pune weather. He's the kind of landlord who exists in a permanent state of mild grievance   the plumbing offends him, the electricity bill wounds him, and the existence of young men in his building is a cross he bears with vocal reluctance.

"Haan, Arjun? Kya chahiye?"

"Good morning, uncle. Bas aise hi   sab theek?"

He narrows his eyes. In his experience, tenants who say "bas aise hi" are about to report a broken pipe, a leaking roof, or a rat the size of a small cat.

"Paani ka bill badhega next month se," he announces, apparently to the universe. "Tum log itna paani kahan use karte ho? Swimming pool banaya hai kya bathroom mein?"

"Nahi uncle, just normal "

"Aur woh tumhara dost   Rohit   woh raat ko 2 baje gym ka bag phenk ke kya baja raha tha? Neeche tak awaaz aayi. Meri wife ne mujhe uthaya. Meri wife ko neend se uthaana   yeh capital offense hai, Arjun."

He closes the door. From inside: "Kaun tha?" His wife's voice. "Woh Arjun. Accha ladka hai, par uska dost..." The door muffles the rest.

## LOCATION: TAPRI CHAI

### SIDE QUEST: Fix Raju Bhaiya's Phone

**[Trigger: Player talks to Raju after first chai visit]**

The second time I approach the tapri, Raju bhaiya is squinting at his phone with the intensity of a man trying to read the Mahabharata through a kaleidoscope. The screen is cracked   not one clean line but a spiderweb of fractures radiating from the upper left corner, the kind of damage that happens when a phone meets a concrete floor at terminal velocity.

"Raju bhaiya, kya hua phone ko?"

He holds it up mournfully. "Kal subah. Chai bana raha tha, phone pocket mein tha, jhuka toh nikal gaya seedha neeche. Pathar pe gira. Ab screen pe kuch dikhta nahi   sab toota hua lag raha hai."

The phone is a Redmi   two generations old, the budget range, probably bought from the same Sassoon Road shop where every student in Pune has bargained for electronics. I take it gently. The screen is shattered but the digitizer might still work   I can see faint images behind the web of cracks. The LCD isn't bleeding.

"Raju bhaiya, screen replace karana padega. Katraj mein ek phone repair shop hai   Anwar bhai ka. Woh probably ₹800-1000 mein kar dega."

"Itna?" His mustache droops. For Raju bhaiya, ₹1,000 is two days of chai earnings. "Chod de, aise hi chalaunga."

"Wait." I turn the phone in my hands. "Agar main Anwar bhai se baat karun   main uska regular customer hoon, Rohit ka phone bhi maine wahan fix karwaya tha   toh maybe discount mil jaaye. Aur data recovery   tera WhatsApp, contacts, sab safe rahe uske liye main backup le leta hoon abhi."

His eyes light up under the bushy eyebrows. "Tu kar sakta hai? Baba, tujhe toh free chai milegi zindagi bhar."

"Ek hafte ke liye chalega." I grin.

I pull up his phone settings   OTG cable from my laptop bag, connect to my laptop, pull the WhatsApp backup. His contact list is a time capsule   every chai customer from the last five years, saved by order: "Arun 2 cutting," "Pramod special ginger," "VIT boys table 3."

### NPC: RANDOM TAPRI CUSTOMER   Morning Encounter

**[Trigger: Player sits at tapri between 07:00-08:00]**

A man in his forties settles onto the bench beside me   shirt tucked into trousers, ID badge on a lanyard, the uniform of the Pune IT belt. He orders a cutting chai and pulls out his phone.

"Bhai, VIT ka student hai?" He's noticed my t-shirt   the VIT logo faded but visible.

"Haan, CSE third year."

"CSE?" His eyebrows rise. "Mera beta bhi CSE kar raha hai. Second year. Hamesha kehta hai coding boring hai." He shakes his head. "Maine use bola   boring hai toh Google bhi boring lagega jab tujhe reject karenge."

"Coding boring nahi hai, uncle. Professor boring hote hain."

He laughs   a full, chest-deep laugh. "Wahi baat. Mere zamaane mein bhi yahi tha." He extends a hand. "Sudhir. Infosys mein kaam karta hoon. Hinjawadi."

We shake hands. His chai arrives. We drink in companionable silence   two strangers connected by chai, a bench, and the comfortable anonymity of a Pune morning.

"Ladki ka scene hai?" he asks after a minute, having apparently read my face with the accuracy of a senior developer debugging code.

"Complicated hai, uncle."

"Sab ladkiyon ka scene complicated hota hai, beta. Simple hota toh shaadi nahi, placement drive hota."

He finishes his chai, drops a ten-rupee note on the counter, and disappears into the morning crowd. I never see him again.

### NPC: FRUIT VENDOR TAI   Maternal Energy

**[Trigger: Player interacts with fruit_vendor on Katraj street]**

The fruit cart sits at the corner where Katraj Road meets the lane to the PGs   a wooden cart piled with seasonal bounty. Pomegranates (Nashik, red, expensive), bananas (Jalgaon, yellow, dirt cheap), guavas (local, green, the September staple), and a pyramid of sitaphal that announces autumn is approaching.

The vendor is a Marathi woman in her fifties   Tai, everyone calls her, the universal Marathi honorific for an older woman. She's built like she could carry two of these carts and is perpetually wrapped in a nine-yard saree that has seen more Pune monsoons than I've seen birthdays.

"Arjun! Kela le ja. Do rupay ek. Subah subah khali pet   kela sabse accha."

"Tai, mera pet bhi khali hai aur wallet bhi."

She tsks   the specific Maharashtrian tsk that communicates volumes of maternal disapproval. "Tum engineering waale ladke. Sab paise Maggi pe uda dete ho. Le, yeh le." She thrusts two bananas at me. "Free. Amma ko bolna   unka ladka kha nahi raha."

I take the bananas. Peel one on the spot. She watches with satisfaction.

"Ladki theek hai teri?" she asks, because apparently my emotional state is visible from space.

"Kaisi ladki, Tai?"

She grins   all teeth, several gold. "Wahi jiske liye tu raat ko bike pe ghumta hai. Hum sab jaante hain, beta. Katraj mein kuch chhupta nahi."

Nothing hides in Katraj. Not heartbreak, not late-night rides, not the fact that I've lost four kilos since Meera stopped being reachable. The neighborhood knows. The neighborhood always knows.

### NPC: STRAY DOG   Karma Points

**[Trigger: Player has biscuit in inventory + interacts with stray_dog]**

The brown dog is always here   stationed between the tapri and the main road like a customs officer, checking everyone's hands for food. Ribs visible. One ear folded permanently, maybe from a fight, maybe from birth. Tail that wags with an optimism his body hasn't earned.

I crouch. Break the Parle-G into pieces. Hold one out on my palm.

He approaches   cautious, low to the ground, eyes on the biscuit, eyes on me, eyes back on the biscuit. The hierarchy of trust: food first, human second. His nose touches my palm   cold, wet. He takes the biscuit gently. No snap. Just a careful extraction, like a surgeon removing a suture.

Three more pieces. He eats them all. Then he sits beside me, leaning his bony flank against my knee. His fur is rough and warm. For about thirty seconds, I have something that loves me without conditions or surveillance.

"Tujhe bhi koi mil jaaye, bhai," I tell him. He wags.

Nikhil catches me in the corridor after Krishnan's lecture. He's holding his notebook   the legendary Nikhil notebook, color-coded, indexed, with handwriting so precise it looks printed. The engineering department's rumor is that Nikhil's notes are the closest thing VIT has to a religious text.

"Arjun, bhai." He adjusts his glasses   round, wire-framed, making him look like an Indian Harry Potter who chose databases over Quidditch. "Tera notes le liya? Krishnan today covered BCNF ke edge cases   woh exam mein zaroor aayenge."

"Mera notebook..." I flip mine open. Two lines of text. A doodle of a ceiling fan. The number 183 in the margin. "Mera notebook ek crime scene hai."

"Thanks yaar. Tu life-saver hai."

He disappears toward the library, notebook clutched like a sacred text. Behind me, two second-year students whisper: "Woh Nikhil ke notes ki photos le raha tha. Lucky bastard."

### NPC: ANANYA   The Library Girl

**[Trigger: Player visits vit_library]**
**[SIDE QUEST: The Girl at the Library]**

The library is cool. Actually cool   AC that works, a miracle of engineering that the rest of the campus hasn't replicated. I slide into a desk near the CS section, mostly to charge my phone, partly to avoid the humidity.

She's two desks over. Ananya   second year CSE, I know her face from the department, not her story. Dark hair in a ponytail, laptop open, typing with the focused intensity of someone debugging production code at 3 AM. Her screen shows VS Code   Python, from the color scheme. She glances at me. Glances away. Glances back.

"Arjun, right? Third year?"

"Haan."

"I'm Ananya. CSE 2nd year." She pulls her earphone out   one ear, keeping the other in, the universal signal for *I'm willing to talk but have a retreat planned.* "I've seen you in the lab. You wrote that Python sorting visualizer last semester   the one Prof. Krishnan showed in class."

I'd forgotten about that. "Oh. Yeah. That was   just a small thing."

"It wasn't small. The recursive partitioning animation was elegant." She says *elegant* like she means it   not a filler word, a precise technical compliment. "I'm working on something similar   a pathfinding visualizer. But my A-star implementation has a bug I can't find."

She turns her laptop toward me. The code is clean   better organized than most third-year students. The bug takes me forty-five seconds to spot.

"Line 87. Your heuristic function is Manhattan distance, but your grid allows diagonal movement. You need Chebyshev distance, or your A-star will over-explore."

She stares at the screen. Then at me. "Oh my god. I've been staring at this for two days."

"Fresh eyes," I say.

She fixes the line. Runs it. The visualization blooms across the screen   nodes expanding in a beautiful, efficient wave toward the target. She grins   and it's the grin of a coder who's just watched their code work, the grin that transcends gender and age and everything else.

"Thank you. Seriously." She pauses. Chews her lip. "Hey   random question. You know B.J. Medical college, right? Sassoon Road?"

My heart rate spikes. "Why?"

"My cousin's studying there. Third year MBBS. She mentioned that the library there has a rare books section   Sanskrit manuscripts, old texts. I'm into computational linguistics and I thought maybe I could access some Sanskrit corpora for an NLP project."

"What's your cousin's name?"

"Sneha. Sneha Patil. Room 302."

The world contracts. Sneha   Room 302   the room next to Room 304. The room next to Meera.

"I might be able to help," I say carefully. "Let me check with some people."

## LOCATION: SINHAGAD ROAD   EVENING

### NPC: DHABA OWNER   Mountain Road Philosophy

**[Trigger: Player rides to sinhagad_road after 17:00]**

The dhaba sits halfway up the Sinhagad approach   a tin roof, four plastic tables, a kerosene stove, and a view of Pune spread below like a circuit board. The owner is an old man   seventy maybe, white stubble, cloth wrapped around his head, eyes that have watched this valley change from farmland to concrete.

"Chai?" One word. He doesn't need more.

"Haan, baba."

He makes it on the kerosene stove   milk, tea leaves, sugar, ginger, all in one blackened pot. The ritual is unhurried. Up here, time moves differently. The city's tempo doesn't reach this elevation.

"Akela aaya hai?" He pours the chai into a steel glass, slides it across the table.

"Haan."

"Pehle dost ke saath aata tha. Aur ek ladki bhi thi   lambe baal, sundar. Ab akela hai."

He remembers. This man, this dhaba, this mountain   they remember Meera and me. We came here twice. Once on the Pulsar, once in a friend's car, both times laughing, both times convinced the world was a place where things worked out.

"Woh nahi aa sakti abhi," I say.

He nods. Doesn't ask why. In his seventy years he's learned that the reasons people can't be together are infinite and rarely interesting. What matters is the chai, the mountain, and the fact that the boy came back alone.

"Chai pi. Sab theek hoga." He turns back to his stove. That's it. No elaboration. No philosophy. Just chai and faith and the mountain watching over both.

## LOCATION: KATRAJ PG ROOM   EVENING/NIGHT

### SIDE QUEST: Debug Rohit's CAD Project

**[Trigger: rohit_confrontation_done + player in PG room after 19:00]**

Rohit's laptop is open on his bed   the screen showing SolidWorks, a 3D model of... something. It might be a piston. It might be a modern art sculpture. With Rohit's CAD work, it's genuinely hard to tell.

"Dekh, bhai," he says, turning the laptop toward me. "Yeh mera semester project hai. Internal combustion engine ka piston assembly. But rendering karte waqt   dekh."

He hits render. The screen goes black. Then white. Then displays an error message in the specific shade of red that software uses when it's personally insulted by your input.

"Rohit, tera mesh density itni high hai ki render engine ko anxiety ho raha hai."

"Mesh density kya hota hai?"

I stare at him. He stares at me. We are two engineering students from different branches, united by friendship and separated by the fundamental question of whether mechanical engineers understand what "too many polygons" means.

Twenty minutes later, I've optimized his mesh, corrected two dimensional errors that would have made his piston the size of a small building, and fixed a material assignment that had given steel the transparency of glass.

"Bhai," he says, watching the render complete   smooth, correct, the piston gleaming silver on screen. "Tu genius hai."

"Tera project hai, tu genius hai. Main sirf debugger hoon."

He's quiet for a moment. Then: "Ek baat bolun? Aaj canteen mein jo bola   usse related?"

"Bol."

"Ek junior tha   Sameer, second year. Usse B.J. Medical ki library mein ek ladki dikhi. Lambe baal, fair, braid mein. Usse kisi ne bola ki woh Kulkarni hai." He pauses. "Colonel Kulkarni ki ladki."

My hands stop on the keyboard. "Kab?"

"Parso. Tuesday. Library mein thi. Apparently books padh rahi thi   kuch Sanskrit type ka, Sameer ko samajh nahi aaya."

Meera. In the library. Reading Sanskrit. Two days ago.

She's alive. She's functioning. She's in a place I can almost reach.

"Rohit." My voice is steadier than my heartbeat. "Thanks."

"Chal, biryani order kar. Tera Swiggy account use karunga   mere pe coupon nahi hai."
