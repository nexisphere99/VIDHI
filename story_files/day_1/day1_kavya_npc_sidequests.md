### NPC: PRIYA PATIL   Morning Routine Chaos

**[Trigger: Player interacts with priya_bed between 06:00-07:00]**

Priya's alarm goes off at 6:15   a Bollywood song, something from Jawan, the bass thumping through her phone speaker with the subtlety of a construction site. She doesn't wake up. The alarm plays for a full minute and forty seconds before she rolls over, slaps the screen, and opens one eye behind her glasses which she apparently slept in again.

"Kavyaaa." She says my name like it has six syllables, all of them pleading. "Aaj ka day kya hai?"

"Thursday."

"Thursday." She processes this. "Pharmacology ka lecture hai?"

"Haan."

"Ugh." She pulls the blanket over her head. Then throws it off. Then pulls it back. The negotiation between Priya and consciousness is a daily spectacle   a three-act drama performed entirely under a cotton bedsheet.

She emerges eventually. Sits up. Her kurta is twisted, her dupatta has migrated to the floor, and her hair looks like it lost a fight with a ceiling fan. She reaches for her phone   the first gesture, always, before water, before breathing properly, before acknowledging the existence of a world that isn't filtered through a 6.5-inch screen.

"Oh my god, Kavya   Reshma posted her engagement photos. LOOK." She thrusts the phone at me. Instagram. A girl I vaguely recognize in a red lehenga, surrounded by flowers, a man in a sherwani, seventeen hashtags including #SheSaidYes and #ReshKiShaadi. "She's our age. OUR AGE. She's getting married and I can't even get a guy to reply to my story."

"Maybe post fewer ASMR links," I offer.

"ASMR is ART, Kavya. It's not my fault boys don't understand the aesthetic appeal of someone whispering while cutting soap."

She shuffles toward the bathroom, phone in hand, still scrolling. At the door she pauses. "Meera already left? Her bed's empty."

I glance at Meera's bed. Made. Pillow arranged. The manuscript   wherever she's hidden it   invisible. "Bathroom mein hogi."

"Hmm." Priya's eyes narrow. Not suspicion exactly   more like the observational reflex of a girl who's been assigned, whether she admits it or not, to notice things. "She's been waking up super early lately. Kya padh rahi hai woh subah subah?"

"Exams, probably."

"Exams in September? Kaun karta hai?" She shrugs and disappears down the corridor, slippers slapping against the stone floor.

I exhale. The first Priya interaction of the day: survived.

### NPC: MEERA KULKARNI   Mess Hall Whisper

**[Trigger: Player goes to hostel_mess for breakfast/dinner, Meera is present]**

The mess hall at 7:30 AM is a war zone of sleepy girls and disappointing food. The menu   written in chalk on a board that hasn't been updated aesthetically since 1997   announces: Poha. Chai. Bread-butter. The poha is watery. The chai is lukewarm. The bread is from yesterday, or possibly from the last government of Maharashtra.

I collect my steel plate   poha ladled by mess didi with the precision of someone who's portioned food for 200 girls and has exactly zero remaining fucks to give. The poha lands on the plate with a wet slap. I add chai   the cup is steel, dented, the handle hot enough to brand cattle.

Meera is at the corner table. Alone. She eats slowly   her movements careful, deliberate, as if even the act of eating is performed under surveillance. Because it is. Three tables away, Priya is scrolling Instagram while eating bread, but her peripheral vision is a weapon.

I sit beside Meera. Our elbows touch. She leans in   not visibly, just a degree, a fraction, the hostel-calibrated lean of a girl who's learned to have conversations without appearing to have them.

"Tonight," she murmurs. Her lips barely move. "After Priya sleeps."

"I know."

"Are you scared?"

I consider this. "I'm a third-year medical student who hates medicine, with a secret phone, a GitHub under a fake name, and a GPA held together by spite. Fear is my default state."

The corner of her mouth lifts. Not quite a smile   something private, the facial equivalent of a handwritten note. "Tonight, Kavya."

She finishes her poha. Washes her plate at the station. Walks away with the posture of a Bharatanatyam dancer   spine straight, shoulders back, every step a controlled performance. The Colonel wanted a daughter who looked perfect. He got one. He just didn't realize the perfection was a disguise.

## LOCATION: HOSTEL BATHROOM

### INTIMATE: Kavya's Pee Scene   Hostel Stall

**[Trigger: Player selects toilet_stall in hostel_bathroom]**
**[INTIMATE SCENE   Body Awareness]**

The stall. Third one from the left   the only one whose flush works consistently and whose bolt actually reaches the latch, although reaching is generous. The bolt is bent. It hooks into the slot at an angle that requires a specific jiggle   push, lift, slide   that I've perfected over three years.

I hike the salwar up, push the panty down to my knees, and sit. The seat is cold   always cold, always slightly damp from the last user or from Pune's ambient moisture, and I've long stopped thinking about which.

The act itself: mundane. Urinary stream, controlled, directed downward by anatomy I didn't design. The sound against porcelain   a small humiliation of shared bathrooms, the knowledge that the girl in the next stall can hear the exact cadence and duration of your bladder's output. I pee quickly. Efficiently. Wipe   front to back, always, the muscle memory of a lesson learned from Amma at age five and never questioned since. Toilet paper is a luxury this hostel doesn't provide   I carry tissues in my kameez pocket, the small packet from the stationery shop on Sassoon Road.

One foot braces against the door   the bolt can't be trusted entirely, and the nightmare of the door swinging open mid-pee is universal to every woman who's ever used a hostel bathroom. The foot presses. The door holds. The vulnerability is brief but real   salwar bunched at my hips, underwear at my knees, body exposed to the specific physics of sitting down to urinate.

Done. Tissue disposed. Panty pulled up   the elastic snaps against my hip. Salwar retied. Flush   the handle requires two attempts, the water swirls sluggishly, job done.

I wash my hands at the sink. The soap dispenser is empty   it's been empty since July. I use the bar soap wedged on the counter, its surface bearing the compressed fingerprints of fifty girls. Wash. Dry on the kameez because the paper towel holder is decorative fiction.

This. This entire process   the stall jiggle, the cold seat, the foot on door, the wipe, the tissue from pocket, the broken soap dispenser. All of it. I've done it four to six times daily for three years. I've never once thought about it as anything other than maintenance.

In three days, I won't have to do any of it.

I don't know that yet. But my body knows something is about to change. The hairs on my forearms are standing.

### NPC: DR. ANIL SHARMA   Post-Dissection Spot Quiz

**[Trigger: Player completes anatomy dissection scene]**
**[SIDE QUEST: Ace Dr. Sharma's Spot Quiz]**

Dr. Sharma circulates between tables after the main dissection. He pauses at mine   fingers clasped behind his back, glasses catching the fluorescent light, the benevolent inquisitor of B.J. Medical's anatomy department.

"Miss Iyer." He uses the formal address   his respect for students is old-school, each name preceded by its honorific regardless of how many times the student has looked like they wanted to vomit on his shoes. "Your brachial plexus dissection is technically excellent. The nerve isolation is clean."

"Thank you, sir."

"Let's test the knowledge behind the hands, shall we?" He smiles   the smile of a man who believes quizzes are gifts. "Name the five terminal branches of the brachial plexus. In order."

My brain switches to retrieval mode   the same mode that fetches Python syntax and LeetCode patterns, only pointed at anatomy instead of algorithms.

"Musculocutaneous nerve. Median nerve. Ulnar nerve. Radial nerve. Axillary nerve."

"Mnemonics?"

"My Mother Understands Rad Anatomy, sir. But honestly I just memorized the list."

He chuckles. "And the roots of the musculocutaneous nerve?"

"C5, C6, C7. Lateral cord."

"What does it innervate?"

"Biceps brachii, brachialis, and coracobrachialis. Sensory   lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm."

He nods. Behind his glasses, his eyes are warm   genuinely warm, the warmth of a man who chose teaching because he believes knowledge is sacred and students are its custodians.

"Miss Iyer, your knowledge is impeccable. Your technique is precise. I only wish..." He pauses. Choosing words. "I only wish I saw the same fire in your eyes that I see in Miss Kulkarni's. Meera touches the cadaver with reverence. You touch it with expertise. Both are valuable. But one of them suggests a calling."

The word *calling* lands like a stone in still water. Ripples outward. He's kind. He means well. He sees a brilliant student and can't understand why she dissects with the enthusiasm of someone filing tax returns.

"Every student finds their fire at their own pace, sir."

"Indeed they do. Indeed they do." He moves to the next table. I exhale.

The fire he wants to see in me is burning. Just not in this building. Not in this body's assigned role.
