## LOCATION: HOSTEL ENTRANCE

### NPC: CHOWKIDAR RAMESH   The First Gate

**[Trigger: Player approaches hostel gate, post-swap]**

Ramesh sits in his booth like a sentry whose war ended thirty years ago but nobody told him. Newspaper folded to the crossword. Thermos of chai. A transistor radio murmuring morning news in Marathi. He's fifty-something, wiry, the mustache of a man who peaked aesthetically in 1995 and has maintained the look since.

I approach the gate. Each step is deliberate   Kavya's stride, short, efficient, minimal hip sway (I've overcorrected; real Kavya has a natural sway I'm suppressing out of terror). The dupatta is pinned. The hair is behind my ears. The chappals slap against cold pavement.

Ramesh looks up. His eyes do a scan   the habitual security check that isn't security so much as the automated recognition of a face that passes him four times a day.

"Good morning, Kavya didi."

My heart   Kavya's heart, faster RPM than mine   lurches. He said the name. HER name. Directed at ME. And his face shows nothing unusual. No squint, no double-take, no slow narrowing of recognition. I'm Kavya to him. The disguise is the body itself.

"Good morning," I manage. Kavya's voice comes out naturally   the vocal cords shaping the Hindi with her Tamil undertone, the pitch correct, the cadence right. The body is performing even while the mind screams.

He returns to his crossword. Seven across. I'm past. I'm IN.

The sign-in register sits on the booth's ledge. I reach for the pen. My hand   Kavya's hand   picks it up and I realize, with a jolt of cold terror, that I have to write. Kavya's handwriting is round, flowing, distinctly feminine. My handwriting is angular, engineering-block. If I sign this register in my handwriting, it's a forensic evidence trail.

I write: *K. Iyer   6:04 AM.* Slowly. Deliberately. Rounding the K, curving the I. The result is   passable. Not Kavya's handwriting, but not mine either. Something in between that could be attributed to morning grogginess.

Ramesh doesn't look at the register. He never does. The register is a ritual, not a security measure. I exhale.

## LOCATION: HOSTEL CORRIDOR & BATHROOM

### NPC: RANDOM HOSTEL GIRL   Corridor Encounter

**[Trigger: Player walks through hostel corridor between 06:00-07:00]**

A girl exits Room 302   Sneha's room   in a towel. Wet hair dripping onto bare shoulders, the towel wrapped from armpits to knees, feet bare on the stone floor. She's carrying a soap dish and a shampoo bottle and the specific murderous expression of a woman who has been denied hot water.

"Oye Kavya, bathroom khali hai agar tujhe chahiye."

I nearly walk into the wall. My eyes   Kavya's eyes   are physically incapable of NOT registering what they're seeing: a girl in a towel, the upper curve of her breasts visible above the terry cloth, the bare skin of her shoulders, the water droplets running down her collarbone. My male brain, transplanted into female hardware, sends conflicting signals. The eyes see a nearly-naked woman. The body doesn't respond the way my body would   no tightening in the groin, no rush of blood southward, because there's no equipment to rush to. Instead: a warmth in the lower abdomen. Diffuse. Confusing.

"Haan, thanks," I manage, eyes averted so hard I'm essentially walking sideways.

She doesn't notice. In the girls' hostel, near-nudity in corridors is as unremarkable as breathing. These girls have been seeing each other's bodies for three years. The towel girl thinks nothing of it. She has no idea that the brain behind Kavya's eyes has never processed female nudity from this proximity without the mediation of a screen.

Another girl passes   this one in a nightie, braless, the outline of everything visible through thin cotton. She nods at me   "Hi Kavya"   and disappears into the bathroom.

I press my back against the wall. Breathe. Kavya's lungs   smaller, the breath coming faster. The corridor is a minefield of female bodies in various states of undress, and I have to navigate it without staring, without blushing, without doing anything that Kavya wouldn't do.

Kavya wouldn't bat an eye. She's seen these bodies for three years. She's shared showers. She's borrowed bras. The female body is not a spectacle to her   it's the default, the unremarkable, the same.

I push off the wall. Walk to Room 304. Keep my eyes forward. Don't look. Don't look. DON'T LOOK.

## LOCATION: HOSTEL MESS

### NPC: MESS DIDI SAVITA   Eating as Kavya

**[Trigger: Player goes to hostel_mess for breakfast]**

Mess didi ladles poha onto my steel plate with the precision of a woman who's been serving institutional food since before liberalization. The poha lands with the wet slap I've heard described but never experienced.

"Kavya! Aaj zyada kha. Tu bahut patli ho rahi hai." She adds an extra spoonful. Unsolicited. Non-negotiable.

I take the plate. The chai   lukewarm, institutional, the flavor of obligation. I sit at the corner table. Eat.

The eating is different. Kavya's body processes food differently   the taste buds register more subtlety. The poha, which I'd have labeled "generic" in my body, has identifiable spice notes: cumin, turmeric, the faint sweetness of onion. The chai has a cardamom undertone I wouldn't have noticed with my palate.

Meera slides in beside me. "How's the food?"

"I can taste individual spices. In my body, food is either 'good' or 'edible.' In this body, it's a full sensory report."

She grins. "Higher olfactory receptor density. Women have more taste buds too. Welcome to the flavor expansion pack."

Priya joins the table. Steel plate, two pieces of bread, jam. Phone in one hand, Instagram loaded. She glances at me. "Kavya, tu aaj kuch alag dikh rahi hai."

My blood   Kavya's blood   turns to ice water.

"Kaise alag?" I ask. Casual. Level. Kavya's deadpan delivery.

"Tera face... relaxed hai. Like kuch accha hua hai." She shrugs. "Or maybe the poha isn't terrible today. Same thing."

She returns to Instagram. The moment passes. But the observation   *tu aaj alag dikh rahi hai*   sits in my gut like swallowed glass.

Priya NOTICES things. She's been placed here to notice things. And today, the thing she's noticing is that "Kavya" is different. Not wrong   different. Calmer. Less tense. The posture slightly off. The way I hold the spoon (left-handed instinct corrected to Kavya's right hand). The way I eat (bigger bites than Kavya would take).

I slow down. Smaller bites. Right hand. Less eye contact with Meera. Perform. Perform. Perform.

## LOCATION: ANATOMY HALL

### NPC: DR. SHARMA   Handwriting Test

**[Trigger: During anatomy lecture, Dr. Sharma circulates]**

Dr. Sharma pauses at my table. The kind eyes, the thick glasses, the paternal warmth that makes me want to confess everything because surely this man would understand that I've swapped bodies with his student and I'm just trying to survive long enough to hold my girlfriend's hand.

"Miss Iyer." He peers at my notebook. The notes I've been copying from Sneha   medical terminology I don't understand, written in handwriting I've been attempting to feminize.

"Your notes today are... different. More organized than usual, actually. New pen?"

Organized because I don't understand the content so I'm copying character-by-character with the deliberation of a calligrapher, rather than Kavya's rapid shorthand of a student who knows the material and abbreviates accordingly.

"Yes sir, new pen. Trying to be more neat." I angle the notebook away. Casual. Not evasive. The angle of someone protecting their work, not hiding evidence.

He nods. Studies me for one more second   a second that lasts approximately six years   and moves on.

I realize I've been gripping the scalpel so hard that Kavya's knuckles are white. I release. The hand trembles.

### SIDE QUEST: The Dropped Specimen Jar

**[Trigger: During anatomy lab, environmental event]**

A crash. Three tables away, a girl   first-year, observing, the terrified face of someone who should not be holding glass containers near slippery surfaces   drops a specimen jar. It shatters. Formalin-preserved tissue spatters across the stone floor. The first-year's face crumbles toward tears.

Dr. Sharma's expression shifts   not angry but concerned. The lab attendant moves to clean up.

I'm on my feet before I decide to be. Kavya's body moves   the response isn't medical instinct (I don't have any) but human instinct. The girl is upset. I help. I cross the three tables, crouch beside the mess, and help the lab attendant collect the largest pieces of glass while another student mops the formalin.

"Theek hai," I tell the first-year. In Kavya's voice, gentler than I expected. "Hota hai. Pehle saal mein sabke saath hota hai."

She sniffles. Nods. Wipes her eyes.

Dr. Sharma watches this from across the room. His expression is   complicated. He's seen Kavya in this lab for three years. He's seen her technical precision. But he's never seen her comfort a student with warmth. The empathy is new. It registers.

"Miss Iyer," he says later, at the end of the lab. "That was kind. You showed the human side of medicine today. I'm proud of you."

I nod. Accept the praise. Walk out.

In the corridor, Meera catches my arm. "That was you," she whispers. "Kavya wouldn't have done that. She'd have continued dissecting."

"Is that a problem?"

"Not a problem. Just different. Dr. Sharma noticed different. Different is risk."

She's right. But the first-year stopped crying. And sometimes being caught is worth being kind.

## LOCATION: ROOM 304   AFTERNOON

### NPC: PRIYA   The Afternoon Threat

**[Trigger: Priya returns to room between 14:00-16:00]**

Priya enters carrying her laptop, two textbooks, and the weight of a woman who's been studying pathology and needs to complain about it.

"Kavya, mujhe pathology samajh nahi aa rahi. Apoptosis aur necrosis mein kya farak hai?"

She's asking me a medical question. A MEDICAL QUESTION. In a subject I know nothing about. The question is so simple that Kavya would answer it without looking up from her phone. If I can't answer it, the suspicion spike will be catastrophic.

"Apoptosis is programmed cell death   controlled, orderly. Necrosis is uncontrolled   trauma, infection." Thank god for high school biology. Thank god for the one time Meera explained this during our earlier briefing.

"Hmm." Priya writes something. Then: "But what's the morphological difference? Like under microscope?"

Deeper. She's going deeper. I have nothing.

"Apoptosis mein cell shrink hoti hai. Necrosis mein cell swell hoti hai." I'm guessing. Educated guessing based on the etymology   *apo* (away), *necro* (death). Programmed death = orderly = shrinking. Uncontrolled death = chaotic = swelling.

"Makes sense. Thanks." She returns to her textbook.

I exhale. The answer was apparently correct   or at least correct enough. CS logic applied to medical terminology. Pattern recognition across domains. The one transferable skill between my world and Kavya's.

### NPC: MEERA   Manuscript Organization (Side Quest)

**[Trigger: priya_absent, meera_available, sq_manuscript_organize]**

Meera spreads the manuscript on her bed   carefully, palm leaves arranged in sequence, the Sanskrit text visible in the fading afternoon light. The text is old, the ink faded, the language from a century I can't imagine.

"I need to organize the translation," she says. "The ritual sections, the theory sections, the warnings. Right now it's all jumbled in my notebook."

I look at the notebook. Her translation   Sanskrit to Hindi, with English annotations. The handwriting is Meera's   precise, dancer's penmanship. The content is   fascinating, even to a CS student. The manuscript describes consciousness as a substrate, transferable between physical containers. The mantra creates a resonance that weakens the bond between consciousness and body. The kumkum circle acts as a boundary condition. The thread is a channel.

"This reads like a system architecture document," I say. "Inputs, processes, outputs. You've got the ritual section   that's the API. The theory section   that's the documentation. And the warnings..."

"The warnings are the error-handling."

We look at each other. She grins. I grin   Kavya's grin, which I'm told is rare and worth seeing.

I help her reorganize. Two hours. We create a structured document   sections numbered, cross-referenced, the warnings highlighted in red ink. The work is satisfying in a way I didn't expect   the organization of ancient knowledge using modern frameworks. The intersection of her Sanskrit and my systems thinking.

"You know," Meera says, as we finish, "you're different in her body."

"Different how?"

"Softer. More present. When you look at me " She pauses. "Kavya looks at me like a friend she's protecting. You look at me like I'm the only thing in the room."

"You are."

She kisses my forehead. Kavya's forehead. The beauty mark under her lips.

I feel it everywhere.
