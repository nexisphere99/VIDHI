## LOCATION: KATRAJ PG — MORNING

### NPC: PG CHAI CIRCLE — Performing Masculinity

**[Trigger: Player goes downstairs to common area, 07:00-08:00]**

The PG's common area is a landing between the stairs and the front door — four plastic chairs, a portable gas stove, and the improvisational chai-making operation of four engineering students who've collectively decided that ₹15 for Raju bhaiya's chai is extravagant when they can produce something inferior for ₹3.

The lanky one with spectacles — Amit, I think, second year IT — waves. "Oye Arjun, chai peega?"

I nod. Take the steel glass. The chai is sweet, oversteeped, the milk slightly curdled. It tastes like a chemistry experiment that achieved sentience and begged for mercy. I drink it.

"Bhai, kitne baje aaya subah?" Another boy — stocky, shaved head, the energy of a man whose primary personality trait is going to the gym. "4:45 pe meri alarm baji — tera Pulsar ki awaaz aayi."

"Morning walk," I say. Arjun's voice, Arjun's casual disregard for punctuality and explanation.

"Morning walk." He exchanges a look with Amit. "Deshmukh morning walk karne laga. Next what — Rohit vegetarian banega?"

Laughter. I join — not my laugh, but Arjun's laugh, which is deeper, less sharp, the kind of laugh that exists more in the chest than the throat. The sound is still novel. Every time this body produces sound, I'm a tourist speaking a foreign language.

I stand with them. Sipping chai. And I perform — legs apart (not crossed, never crossed, men don't cross their legs standing), one hand in the jeans pocket (the left, Arjun's habit, I've observed), the easy half-lean against the wall. Weight on one foot, hip cocked slightly — no. Not HIP COCKED. That's mine. Kavya's hip-cock. I straighten. Both feet. Wider stance.

Nobody questions it. I'm invisible in the way Arjun is invisible — a boy in a boys' PG, drinking chai, existing without annotation. No one is monitoring my dupatta placement because there's no dupatta. No one is checking the tightness of my clothes because a t-shirt and jeans on a male body invites exactly zero commentary. No one cares how I stand or sit or hold my cup.

This is what it feels like to not be watched. The sensation is so alien and so intoxicating that I almost forget to drink the terrible chai.

### NPC: AMIT — Laptop Virus (Side Quest)

**[Trigger: Amit mentions laptop problem during chai circle]**
**[SIDE QUEST: Fix Amit's Laptop]**

"Arjun bhai, ek problem hai." Amit adjusts his spectacles — the universal gesture of a man about to ask for free tech support. "Mera laptop... kuch ho gaya hai. Pop-ups aa rahe hain. Browser pe ads. Speed chudi padi hai. Virus lagta hai."

"Dikha," I say. One word. Arjun's economy of speech for tech matters — fewer words, more action.

Amit brings his laptop — an HP, two years old, the keyboard missing three keycaps, the screen smudged with fingerprints that tell the story of every assignment and every late-night browsing session. He opens it.

The virus is immediately obvious to my trained eye — browser hijacker malware, probably from a cracked software download. Toolbar extensions injecting ads, a startup entry redirecting the home page, registry modifications hiding the process from Task Manager.

I open Command Prompt. Amit watches with the awe of a man witnessing surgery. I navigate to system folders, identify the malicious executables, kill the processes, remove the registry entries. Then browser — clear extensions, reset settings, remove cached malware. The whole operation takes twelve minutes.

"Bhai." Amit stares at the now-clean laptop. "Tu toh normally itna technical nahi karta. Aaj kya hua?"

Shit. Arjun probably would have used a simple antivirus scan. I just performed a manual malware removal that requires command-line knowledge beyond his demonstrated skill level. Another brilliance leak.

"YouTube tutorial," I say. The universal excuse. "Kal raat dekha tha."

Amit accepts this. "Bhai, thanks. Tu genius hai. Biryani khila dunga."

The biryani is never coming. But the trust is banked — Amit will now vouch for Arjun's presence in the PG, which helps cover any future absences.

"Sure, Raju bhaiya. Milwa dena."

"Kal aayega. Subah 8 baje. Yahan. Chai ke saath meeting." He beams. "Raju ka tapri — India ka naya LinkedIn."

### NPC: ROHIT — Canteen Lunch (The Extended Test)

**[Trigger: Player goes to canteen with Rohit after lecture]**

**[IMAGE: kavya_rohit_canteen.jpg — INSERT HERE]**

The canteen. Rohit drops a plate of dosa in front of me — his greeting ritual, apparently, the food-as-love-language of a Punjabi boy whose affection comes in carbohydrates.

"Bhai, KAUNSA YouTube channel?"

I knew this was coming. The Krishnan incident has been percolating in Rohit's brain for two hours, and Rohit's brain, when sufficiently agitated, produces confrontations with the inevitability of monsoon.

"Random one. Algorithm analysis. Late night recommendation."

"Random one." He tears his dosa. Chews. His eyes don't leave my face. "Arjun, tujhe Red-Black trees ka Sedgewick optimization kab se aata hai? Tera DBMS assignment mein tu basic joins nahi kar pa raha tha."

"DBMS alag hai. Algorithms alag."

"Algorithms mein bhi — bhai, tu kal tak Krishnan ke questions se bachta tha. Aaj tu uth ke answer de raha hai jaise tu Stanford se transfer aaya ho."

This is dangerous. Rohit is close — closer to the truth than he knows. The behavioral shift is too dramatic. Arjun from two days ago vs. "Arjun" today — the delta is visible, and Rohit is the person most calibrated to detect it.

I deploy the one weapon I have: emotional deflection.

"Rohit." I lower my voice. Set down the dosa. Make eye contact — Arjun's eyes, which I've learned carry a particular quality of earnest sincerity. "Meera ke baare mein jo call aaya tha na? It motivated me. I've been wasting time. Six months pining and not doing anything. The call was a wake-up."

The truth, wrapped in a lie, delivered in his best friend's voice. The manipulation makes me uncomfortable — Rohit is genuine and I'm exploiting his trust. But the alternative is exposure, and exposure kills the swap.

Rohit studies me. His jaw works through dosa and suspicion simultaneously. Then: "Agar Meera ki wajah se tu padhai kar raha hai — finally — toh I support it. Par bhai, itna sudden change... log notice karenge."

"Main dhyan rakhunga."

"Rakh. Because Krishnan ne tujhe aise dekha jaise tu alien hai. Which, honestly, you might be."

He grins. The tension breaks. I grin back — Arjun's grin, which I've practiced in the bathroom mirror until it no longer looks like a grimace.

### NPC: PROF. KRISHNAN — Post-Lecture Corridor

**[Trigger: After DS lecture, player walks through C-Block corridor]**

"Arjun."

I turn. Krishnan is walking behind me — he must have followed from the lecture hall. His expression is — not suspicious exactly. Curious. The curiosity of a professor who's just witnessed a student perform beyond their established ceiling.

"Sir?"

"That solution. The lazy propagation approach to double-black resolution. I published a paper in 2022 that used a similar technique for AVL trees. Have you read it?"

My heart — Arjun's heart, larger, slower, but currently racing — pounds.

"No sir," I say. True — I haven't read THAT paper. I've read a different one, the BST optimization paper, which is how I know his work. But I can't reference it specifically because Arjun wouldn't have.

"Interesting." He studies me over his glasses. "Because the approach you described is remarkably similar to my optimization. Not identical — you used amortized analysis where I used aggregate — but the underlying insight is the same."

"Coincidence, sir. I was exploring the problem independently."

"Independent discovery." He nods slowly. "That's either a coincidence or a sign that you've been underperforming in my class for two and a half years. Either way, I'd like you to consider submitting to the department's annual research symposium. The deadline is in three weeks."

A research symposium. A professor is asking me — asking ARJUN — to submit academic research. The invitation is intoxicating. It's also a problem: if this becomes a commitment, Arjun will have to deliver work at MY level in future swaps.

"I'll think about it, sir."

"Think fast. And Arjun—" He pauses at his office door. "Whatever happened between yesterday and today — keep it happening."

He closes the door. I stand in the C-Block corridor, Arjun's body running on Kavya's brain, holding an invitation to academic validation from the professor I've admired for two years through YouTube lectures, and I want to scream. Not in frustration — in the pure, distilled joy of being SEEN for what I can do, even if the body doing it isn't mine.

### NPC: NIKHIL — ICPC Prep Discussion

**[Trigger: Player encounters Nikhil in canteen or lab]**

"Bhai." Nikhil materializes with the inevitability of a merge conflict. "ICPC practice. Saturday. We need to prep."

"What topics?"

"Graph algorithms, dynamic programming, and string processing. Last year's preliminary had a minimum spanning tree problem that eliminated sixty percent of teams."

"Kruskal's or Prim's?"

"Kruskal's with union-find optimization. But the trick was—" He pushes his glasses up. "—the graph had negative weights. Most teams used Kruskal's and got wrong answers. The correct approach was Bellman-Ford for shortest path, then minimum spanning tree on the residual."

I know this. I've solved this exact problem on Codeforces. The solution is elegant — a two-phase algorithm that most competitive programmers miss because they commit to one approach too early.

"I had a similar problem on—" I stop. I was about to say "Codeforces." Arjun isn't on Codeforces. KV_Codes is on Codeforces. "—a practice set I found online."

"Which set?"

"Random. Can't remember the URL."

Nikhil accepts this with the credulity of a man who's too excited about competitive coding to question the source. We spend twenty minutes discussing strategy — his knowledge is solid, methodical, textbook. Mine is — well, Kavya's is — intuitive, pattern-based, the result of three years of unsupervised self-study and the kind of creative problem-solving that formal education can't teach because it's born from constraint.

By the end, Nikhil is looking at me with an expression I've never received from a peer: respect. Technical respect. The kind that says *you know things I don't.*

"Bhai, seriously. Kaunsa YouTube channel?"

"I'll send you the link."

There is no link. There is a girl in a medical hostel who solved these problems at 2 AM on a ₹3,000 phone. But that story isn't available today.

### NPC: CANTEEN ANNA — The Order Test

**[Trigger: Player orders food at canteen]**

Anna — the South Indian uncle behind the dosa griddle — sees me approach. "Arjun! Aaj kya?"

The question is a test I didn't study for. What does Arjun order? Masala dosa? Plain dosa? Idli? I saw his canteen interactions in the game bible — masala dosa, usually. But is it ALWAYS masala dosa?

"Masala dosa, Anna."

"Usual." He ladles batter onto the griddle. The sizzle is correct. The sambar smells correct. The transaction feels correct. Anna slides the plate across.

"Tera dost ka bhi banaun? Rohit? Woh teen khaata hai."

"Haan, usne bola hai." I don't know if Rohit ordered through Arjun or separately. I'm guessing.

Anna starts a second batch. The guess was apparently right — or at least unremarkable. The dosa arrives. I carry two plates to the table. Rohit, arriving, takes one without comment. The food pipeline functions.

But the dosa itself — in Arjun's body, the flavor profile is different from my body's experience. Less nuance. The spices are there but muted — the cumin is cumin, the turmeric is turmeric, but the layers I'd taste in Kavya's body are flattened here. The male palate: functional but lower-resolution.

I eat the entire dosa. My stomach — Arjun's stomach, the metabolism of a twenty-one-year-old male who swims and rides motorcycles — processes it like a furnace receiving fuel. I'm still hungry. In my body, one dosa is sufficient. In this body, one dosa is an appetizer.

I order a second. Anna raises an eyebrow — approvingly. "Aaj bhookh lagi hai. Acchi baat hai."
