Amit sees me and his face lights up   the gratitude of a man whose laptop now works, directed at the person who fixed it. "Arjun bhai! Mera laptop ekdum smooth chal raha hai. Tu toh doctor hai   laptop ka."

The invisibility of maleness wraps around me like a weather system   I'm inside it, I'm carried by it, and the effort of maintaining it decreases with each repetition.

### NPC: ROHIT   The Ongoing Investigation

**[Trigger: Morning in PG room, 07:30 AM, after deodorant incident]**

The Axe cloud has dissipated but the suspicion hasn't. Rohit sits on his cot, protein shake in hand (today's batch is fresh, thank god), watching me with the focused attention of a man building a case he doesn't have the evidence to prosecute.

Chaar: tu baat karte waqt kabhi kabhi alag vocabulary use kar raha hai. Paanch: tu CHAI pee rahi   arey   pee raha hai differently. Pehle tu ek sip mein khatam karta tha. Ab tu small sips le rahi   le raha hai."

He caught himself. Twice. Almost said "pee rahi hai" instead of "pee raha hai"   the feminine verb form slipping into his description of me because at some subconscious level, something about "Arjun" today reads feminine to the part of Rohit's brain that processes gender cues.

The list is devastating. Five data points, each individually dismissible, collectively damning. Rohit Sharma   the man I dismissed as a loud Punjabi gym bro   is performing forensic behavioral analysis with a precision that would earn him marks in a criminology exam.

"Rohit." I deploy the emotional weapon again   the one that worked at lunch yesterday. I set down my chai. Make eye contact. Arjun's earnest-sincerity expression, the one I've practiced in the bathroom mirror. "Meera ke baare mein jo hua   the phone call   it changed something. Main wahi Arjun hoon. Bas... motivated hoon. Finally."

"Motivated se aadmi ka bench press form nahi bhoolna chahiye."

"Stomach kharab tha. I told you."

"Aur smell?"

"New deo try kiya. Didn't work. Back to Axe."

"Aur vocabulary?"

"Kaunsa vocabulary?"

"Tu kal 'synergistic' bola. Canteen mein. While talking about sambar." He stares. "SYNERGISTIC. Arjun Deshmukh. About SAMBAR."

Shit. That was me. My medical-student vocabulary leaking through Arjun's mouth. *Synergistic.* A word Arjun has never used because he lives in a world of functions and loops, not drug interactions and dose-response curves.

"YouTube video dekha tha. Cooking channel. Chef ne scientific terms use kiye the."

"..." Rohit stares for five full seconds. Then picks up his shake. Takes a swig. "Okay." But the "okay" doesn't mean he's convinced. It means he's out of questions for now. The investigation is paused, not closed.

As he leaves for the bathroom, he turns at the door. "Bhai, whoever you're becoming   better or different or whatever   I'm here. But if something is actually wrong... tu bata dena. Main tere saath hoon."

The sincerity hits like a thrown brick wrapped in velvet. This man   this loud, protein-obsessed, Axe-drenched man   loves his best friend with the uncomplicated, unconditional force of someone who doesn't need reasons. He's watching Arjun change. He doesn't understand why. He doesn't like it. But his response isn't suspicion for its own sake   it's concern for his person.

The guilt of deceiving him sits in Arjun's stomach like an undigested meal.

"Main theek hoon, Rohit. Promise."

"Promise ki definition badal gayi hai apparently. But okay."

He closes the bathroom door. The shower starts. I sit on Arjun's bed and breathe.

## LOCATION: VIT CAMPUS

### NPC: PROF. KRISHNAN   Abstract Discussion

**[Trigger: Player encounters Krishnan in corridor or visits his office]**

"Arjun." Krishnan materializes in the C-Block corridor with the inevitability of a professor who's been thinking about you. "The symposium. Have you decided on a topic?"

"Resource-constrained ML inference, sir. Optimizing machine learning models for low-end hardware. Mobile devices in data-poor environments."

The topic is mine. Purely, entirely mine. Born from three years of coding on a ₹3,000 Redmi with 2GB RAM and a processor that considers loading Chrome to be an ambitious request. The optimization techniques I've developed aren't theoretical   they're survival strategies, hacks that squeeze neural network inference out of hardware that wasn't designed for it.

Krishnan's eyebrows lift   the slow, professorial ascent of interest. "Data-poor environments. Like rural healthcare?"

The connection fires. Rural healthcare. Low-end phones. Medical image classification running on devices that cost less than a stethoscope. The ML medical imaging project I've been building on Arjun's laptop   it's exactly this. The convergence of my CS skills and my medical knowledge, married in a topic that serves both.

"Yes sir. Specifically   deploying medical image classifiers on budget Android phones for community health workers who don't have access to radiologists."

"You're describing a real problem." His voice has shifted from interested to ENGAGED. "There's a WHO initiative   mHealth   that's been trying to solve exactly this. The constraint is always the hardware. If you could demonstrate inference on   what   a Snapdragon 400-series processor?"

"Or even a MediaTek G-series. ₹3,000 phone range."

"₹3,000." He does the math. "At that price point, the model would need to be   what   under 5MB? Quantized? Pruned?"

"Quantized to INT8, pruned by 70%, knowledge-distilled from a full ResNet to a custom MobileNet variant. I've been prototyping." The words come fast   Arjun's deep voice carrying my expertise with an authority that Kavya's voice, in Kavya's body, in Kavya's medical college, has never been allowed to express.

Krishnan is quiet for four seconds. Which, in Krishnan-time, is an eon.

"Write the abstract. One page. Submit by Friday. I want to co-author this."

CO-AUTHOR. A professor wants to put his name alongside work that a medical student in a borrowed body is pretending a CS student produced. The academic ethics are a pretzel. The intellectual theft is   except it's not theft, it's MY work, MY brain, just channeled through the wrong body in the wrong department in the wrong life.

"Yes sir."

He walks away. I lean against the C-Block wall and feel the weight of what just happened. Prof. Ravi Krishnan   the man whose BST optimization paper I read on a phone during pharmacology lecture and wept at the beauty of   wants to co-author a paper with me. With "Arjun." With the body I'm wearing for twelve hours.

The joy is incandescent. The complication is catastrophic. This paper, if written, becomes ARJUN'S on the record. His name. His CV. His academic future. The work is mine but the credit will be his because the hands that typed it were his and the face that presented it was his and the name on the university records is his.

Unless.

Unless the swaps don't stop. Unless this body   his body   becomes a permanent residence rather than a temporary visa.

The thought arrives again. Uninvited. Harder to dismiss than yesterday.

### NPC: NIKHIL   ICPC Strategy Session

**[Trigger: Player encounters Nikhil in canteen or lab]**

Nikhil's ICPC preparation is military-grade. He's produced a spreadsheet   a SPREADSHEET   of problem categories, historical frequency in ICPC prelims, their combined strength-weakness analysis, and a study plan color-coded by urgency.

"Graph algorithms   we're both decent. Dynamic programming   you're stronger since yesterday." He pushes his glasses up. "String processing   my strength. Number theory   neither of us."

"Number theory we can skip for prelims. It's a 5% category and we'd waste twenty hours to MAYBE solve one problem."

"Agree. So our strategy: graph first, DP second, string third. Each practice session, three problems, ninety minutes. Discuss after."

We sit in the CS lab   empty at 4 PM, the monitors humming, the AC wheezing. He opens Codeforces. We tackle a graph problem together   minimum cost network flow.

My solution takes eight minutes. Nikhil's takes twelve. His is textbook-correct. Mine is   different. I use a technique I developed on my Redmi, optimizing for memory because the Redmi has 2GB RAM and standard algorithms overflow. The memory optimization happens to also improve time complexity by a constant factor.

"Bhai." Nikhil stares at my code. "Yeh approach kahan se aaya? This isn't in any textbook I've read."

"Personal optimization. When you've got limited hardware, you learn to squeeze."

"LIMITED HARDWARE? Tera laptop i5 hai with 8GB RAM. That's not limited."

"I meant   in theory. Like, thinking about resource-constrained scenarios." The cover is thin. The truth   I developed this on a ₹3,000 phone with 2GB RAM   would blow every cover simultaneously.

He studies my code for another minute. Then: "Arjun, genuinely   if you've been hiding this skill level for two and a half years, you owe the department an apology. And if this is new   if something unlocked in your brain recently   I want to know what it is."

"YouTube," I say. For the twenty-seventh time. The word has become a mantra, a shield, a prayer.

"YouTube." He saves my code to his USB. "I'm going to find this channel if it kills me."

He won't find it. Because the channel is a girl in a medical hostel, coding on a phone, and that's not on YouTube. That's on the encrypted Telegram channel where KV_Codes posts solutions and a maintainer once called them "elegant."

Nobody helps. Nobody offers.

This boy   this gentle, furniture-apologizing, ceiling-fan-staring boy   just washed my bras because they were dirty and his hands were mine and he read the instructions I left on my phone.

Two people wearing each other's skins, caring for each other's bodies, building a strange and specific intimacy that has nothing to do with sex and everything to do with the radical act of maintenance   of washing someone's bra because it needed washing, of hooking someone's bra because it needed hooking, of being responsible for a body that belongs to someone who trusted you with it.
