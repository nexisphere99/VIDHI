### NPC: RAJU BHAIYA — Tapri Wisdom + Tea Supplier

**[Trigger: Morning tapri, 07:15 AM]**
**[SIDE QUEST: Help Raju Find Tea Supplier]**

The cutting chai slides across the counter — automatic, Arjun's order, two years of patronage compressed into a single, wordless transaction. Raju bhaiya's mustache is in philosophical mode today — the tips slightly drooped, the expression of a man with something heavier than chai on his mind.

"Arjun baba, aaj VIT jaana hai?"

"Haan, Raju bhaiya."

"Padhai mein dhyan rakh." He wipes the counter — the meditative motion of a man choosing his words. "Mere bete ne engineering chodi. Accha student tha — 78% aaya tha boards mein. But fees — VIT jaisi college ki fees kahan se laaye? Scholarship nahi mila. Ab Uber chalata hai."

The weight of his words is different through Arjun's ears than it would be through mine. For Arjun, education is obligation — the family investment to be honored, the degree to be completed. For me — for Kavya — education is liberation. The RIGHT education, in the RIGHT field, is the difference between a life of dissecting cadavers and a life of writing code. Raju's son didn't lose education — he lost ACCESS to education. I haven't lost access. I've been given the WRONG access.

"Main dhyan rakhunga, Raju bhaiya."

"Aur ek baat — mera chai supplier, Gupta ji from Kothrud — usne supply band kar di. Paise ka jhagda. Ab mera chai ka stock teen din ka bacha hai. Agar tu koi wholesale supplier dhundh sake — phone pe — toh bahut madad hogi."

A side quest. Not CS, not coding, not algorithms. A SUPPLY CHAIN problem. The chai vendor's upstream logistics have failed and he needs a replacement supplier before his inventory runs out.

I pull out Arjun's phone. Google: "wholesale tea leaf supplier Pune." IndiaMart listings, JustDial results, a wholesaler in Market Yard, another in Shivaji Nagar. I filter by reviews, cross-reference prices, find three options with good ratings and delivery service.

"Raju bhaiya, yeh teen options hain." I show him the screen. "Market Yard wala sabse sasta hai but delivery charge zyada. Shivaji Nagar wala moderate price but free delivery over ₹5,000. Aur yeh online wala — Chaiwala Wholesale — yeh directly Assam se bhejta hai. Premium but authentic."

He stares at the screen. Then at me. Then at the screen again. His mustache achieves a configuration I haven't seen before — both tips lifted in wonder.

"Arjun baba, tu toh chai ka consultant ban gaya."

"Bas Google search kiya, Raju bhaiya."

"Google? Pehle toh tu chai pee ke chala jaata tha. Aaj chai ka business plan bana raha hai. Kuch ho gaya hai tujhe."

The observation is accurate and uncomfortable. Arjun doesn't optimize supply chains for chai vendors. Arjun drinks chai and leaves. The person optimizing logistics is KAVYA — the systematic brain, the girl who built a dosage calculator app for Sneha, who organizes her underwear drawer by day of the week, who approaches every problem as a system to be analyzed and improved.

"Bas, motivated hoon aaj kal."

He accepts. Calls the Shivaji Nagar supplier on the spot. Free delivery. ₹4,800 for a month's supply. Deal closed over the tapri counter while the next batch of chai boils.

"Arjun baba, tujhe free chai — poora MAHINA. Not just ek hafta. POORA MAHINA."

A month of free chai. The ROI on a five-minute Google search is — well, Raju bhaiya's free chai is objectively the best currency available in Katraj's economy.

**[Trigger: After DS lecture, corridor/canteen]**

I hand him Arjun's notebook. The pages show: Arjun's baseline messy handwriting WITH my cleaner annotations scattered between. The hybrid looks like a student who's IMPROVING — not transformed, just... trying harder. Getting marginally better. The slow uplift of someone who's "finally studying."

"Bhai, tera handwriting accha ho raha hai," Nikhil observes, flipping pages. "Pehle doctor jaisi thi. Ab readable hai."

He photographs the relevant pages. Then: "Krishnan ne bonus problem post kiya — class portal pe. RB-tree deletion edge case. Delete a node with two children where the replacement is also red. I know this problem. I've solved variants on three competitive coding platforms. The solution is: after replacement, check for the red-red violation, then apply a single rotation and recoloring. The optimal implementation handles it in O(1) additional operations.

But the solution must look ARJUN-LEVEL. Include one deliberate mistake. Submit. Then "correct" it in a revised submission — simulating the learning process, the try-fail-learn cycle that professors find more convincing than perfection.

"Main attempt karunga. Tujhe solution bhejun?"

"Bhej. Agar sahi hai toh." He grins — the Nikhil grin, competitive and collegial simultaneously. "Aur agar galat hai toh AT LEAST we'll get partial marks together."

I solve it that evening on Arjun's laptop. Version 1: correct approach, one deliberate error in the rotation direction (left instead of right after the replacement). Submit. Version 2: corrected, with a comment "fixed rotation bug." Submit. The two-submission trail looks like learning. Krishnan will see it and think: Arjun tried, failed, learned, succeeded. The PATTERN of improvement.
